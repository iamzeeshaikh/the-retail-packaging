Print method is decided by arithmetic more often than by quality. One route carries a fixed setup cost and low running cost; another carries no setup and higher running cost. Where those curves cross depends on quantity, colour count and finishing.

## The five methods

| Method | Setup | Efficient run | Colour | Substrates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Digital | None | 50 – 2,000 | Good, process build | Board, film, labels |
| Offset litho | Plate per colour | 2,000+ | Excellent, spot native | Flat board, wraps, labels |
| Flexography | Plate per colour | 2,500+ | Good on solids | Corrugated, film, bags |
| Rotogravure | Cylinders, high cost | 10,000+ | Excellent | Flexible film |
| Screen | Screen per colour | 100+ | Heavy ink lay | Fabric, thick stocks |

## Digital printing

No plates, so no setup cost to amortise. Unit price is close to flat regardless of quantity, which makes it the correct answer for short runs, prototypes, seasonal variants and market tests.

Its unique capability is versioning. Ten variants of one carton print in a single run at no extra setup, because there are no plates to change.

It also removes several days from the schedule, which matters when a deadline is fixed.

## Offset lithography

Ink transfers from plate to blanket to substrate. One plate per colour, and once colour is running to target it produces sheets fast and consistently.

Best colour accuracy of the board methods, and spot colours print directly from named inks rather than being approximated. Holds fine detail in small type better than any alternative.

## Flexography

Plate-based, used mainly on corrugated, film and label stock. Handles solid colours and line work well; struggles with fine gradients and photographic detail on fluted or absorbent surfaces.

Where photographic quality is needed on corrugated, litho-lamination prints a paper sheet offset and bonds it to the board. Better result, higher cost.

## Rotogravure

Engraved cylinders, very high setup cost, outstanding quality and speed at volume. Only viable on flexible film runs above roughly ten thousand units.

## Screen printing

Ink pushed through a mesh screen. Lays down a thick ink film, which is why it suits fabric, thick rigid wraps and applications needing heavy opaque coverage. Each colour needs its own screen and pass.

## Finding your crossover

Ask for the same specification quoted digitally and plate-based, at your quantity and at the next band up. Four numbers reveal the shape of both curves.

Be honest about the quantity you will use rather than the one that produces the best unit price. Stock that becomes obsolete before it is used is not a saving.

Where a run sits near the crossover, factor in the probability of a design change. Flexibility has a value that does not show in the unit price, and [the underlying cost mechanics](/resources/packaging-cost-factors/) explain why the curve bends where it does.
