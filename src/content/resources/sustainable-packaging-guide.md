Three distinct claims get treated as one, and that is where brands attract complaints. This guide separates them and sets out what each option genuinely delivers.

## The three claims

**Recycled content** — what the material is made from. Post-consumer recycled fibre has been used and recovered; pre-consumer is manufacturing offcuts. State the percentage.

**Recyclability** — whether local infrastructure accepts it. Plain corrugated and uncoated paperboard are accepted almost everywhere. Multi-layer laminated film is accepted almost nowhere.

**Compostability** — breakdown under specific conditions, almost always industrial rather than home. Home-compostable certification exists but is much rarer.

## Options compared

| Option | Cost vs conventional | Performance | Real-world recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recycled paperboard | 0 – 5% higher | Near-equivalent | Kerbside, widely accepted |
| Recycled corrugated | Often standard | Equivalent | Kerbside, widely accepted |
| Aqueous coating | Broadly neutral | Lower barrier than laminate | Keeps paper recyclable |
| PE-free grease barrier | 5 – 15% higher | Good for short contact | Keeps paper recyclable |
| Mono-material PE film | 10 – 20% higher | Close to laminate | Store drop-off only |
| Certified compostable | 20 – 40% higher | Adequate, shorter shelf life | Industrial facilities only |
| Moulded pulp | Comparable to foam | Good impact absorption | Kerbside, widely accepted |

## Where each option falls short

Recycled board has a greyer base tone and slightly shorter fibre, so very high recycled grades are marginally less rigid at the same thickness.

Aqueous coatings do not match laminate for gloss or moisture barrier. For wet or long-contact applications they are not a drop-in replacement.

Mono-material films need filling line adjustment — seal temperatures and dwell times change. Trial before converting a live SKU. Store drop-off is also a much weaker recovery route than kerbside.

Certified compostable materials require industrial facilities that most US consumers cannot access, and they contaminate recycling streams when they end up there.

## The change that actually reduces material

Right-sizing removes board, reduces shipping volume and cuts freight emissions in one move — and lowers cost rather than raising it. It is the only option here that improves both the environmental position and the budget.

Eliminating a component does the same. Designing for a single material stream helps too: a carton that is entirely paper recycles cleanly, while the same carton with a plastic window and a foil block does not.

## Writing a defensible claim

Answer three questions before printing anything: what specifically is claimed, what evidence supports it, and what should the customer do with the pack.

"Recycle with paper" and "industrially compostable, not suitable for home composting" are defensible. A generic leaf symbol is not.

Certification documentation and stated recycled percentages turn a marketing statement into a substantiated one, and the [material options behind each claim](/resources/packaging-material-guide/) set out what is actually achievable at each price point.
