Work through this before committing a packaging run. Most production problems are caught by one of these checks, and all of them are cheaper to resolve before tooling than after.

## Structure

- Product measured at its widest point in three dimensions, in the state it will be packed
- Product weight recorded
- Clearance allowed per side, not per dimension
- Board grade specified against weight, not against appearance
- Decision made on whether an insert is required
- Format confirmed as suitable for the product weight and journey
- Plain structural sample ordered and tested with the real product

## Compliance and content

- All legally required panel content collected at final length
- Ingredient, allergen, INCI or supplement facts panel laid out before decorative design
- State-specific warnings confirmed for every market where the product is sold
- California Proposition 65 requirement checked if applicable
- Net weight and country of origin present
- Barcode type and number confirmed
- Compliance sign-off obtained from whoever is responsible, in writing

## Artwork

- Design built on the supplied dieline, not a redrawn rectangle
- Bleed applied to every trim edge at the correct distance for the format
- Critical content held inside safety margins
- No text or barcode crossing a fold
- Colour mode CMYK, spot colours named consistently
- Rich black build used on large solids, 100% K on small text
- Images 300 dpi at placed size
- Fonts outlined or embedded
- Dieline on a separate non-printing layer
- Total ink coverage within press limit

## Retail requirements

- Retailer planogram dimensions confirmed in writing where applicable
- Case identification present on at least two adjacent faces
- Opening method tested by someone who has not seen it before
- Tray appearance checked with half the product removed
- Stacking strength specified against pallet load, not contents weight
- Barcode scan-tested on the actual printed substrate

## Commercial and logistics

- Quantity based on realistic consumption, not on reaching a price break
- Price obtained at your quantity and the next band up
- Tooling position confirmed — who holds the die, and what a reorder costs
- Lead time confirmed in writing, including transit
- Delivery address receiving constraints checked, including 3PL rules
- Storage conditions considered for heat and humidity
- Reorder trigger point calculated from lead time and consumption

## Before signing off the proof

- Proof checked against a written specification, not from memory
- Correct artwork version confirmed
- Physical sample reviewed if fit or colour is critical
- Approval given by someone authorised to give it

Once production completes, archive the approved print file, dieline, board specification, finish list and colour targets together. Reorders that reuse them are faster, cheaper and more consistent, as set out in our [guide to ordering packaging in volume](/resources/wholesale-order-guide/).
